How the rover drives from B to I

A LiDAR, ultrasonic and gyro box on the WAVE ROVER that follows a wall, finds a turn landmark, crosses an open square on fixed landmarks, follows a second wall and a railing corridor, and stops at the glass entrance of Block I. No GPS, no camera, rover firmware unchanged.

~160 mB to I: 20 m wall, 40 m open sea, 10 m arcade, 60 m interlock and railings
10 logsmission logs measured with the same code the box runs

The route in plain words

Every leg uses a different thing it can see, because no single reference exists along the whole way.

1
Leg 1, along the B facade (about 20 m). The wall is on the left, 1.1 to 1.6 m away. The box keeps that distance and stays parallel to it. Pillars stand on the right. After a gap in the facade the turn pillar comes, with a dark sign structure behind it.
2
First right turn. The box finds the sign structure beside the turn pillar, drives 0.4 m past the pillar edge and pivots 90 degrees to the right, perpendicular to the B wall.
3
Leg 2, the Open Sea (about 40 m). There are no walls, only a row of trees, stakes and posts on the left, then a building front, then the far end wall. The box steers on those, and on the B wall behind it at the start.
4
Leg 3 and the left turn T3. At the end wall it turns right and follows the arcade wall on its left at 0.6 m. When that wall ends (after about 10 m) it turns left around the corner.
5
Leg 4 to Block I. It follows the next wall at 1.0 m, crosses the light interlock walkway, enters the corridor between two railings, climbs the slight ramp and stops 1.5 m after the recessed glass entrance (KARL).
In one sentence: the rover reads the shape of the campus with its LiDAR, uses the gyro to hold a straight line in between, and stops and waits whenever something is in front of it.

Safety rules that never change

  • Turns need full power (0.50, a step to full like Kethan's code); forward driving is slow (0.22, leg 4 0.30).
  • Every stop is a ramp. Normal 0.30 per second, obstacle 0.80 per second, never a jump to zero.
  • People in front: stop and wait. No swerving. After a hold the rover lines up again and retries.
  • Only STOP and a real tilt pause. A lost rover link makes it stand still and continue.
  • The rover firmware is not touched. The box only sends T:1 and reads T:130 and T:139.
  • The ESP32 is not stressed. No flash erase from the program, no heavy Wi-Fi traffic.
  • Flash at 115200, never Erase All Flash.
  • Obstacles are verified. A near LiDAR point needs 4 beams or the front ultrasonic; an obstacle that vanishes from closer than 0.45 m is still treated as there.

Open points and next field test

  • Drive v10 on the whole route and check: turn at the sign pillar from a different start, stop at the KARL entrance.
  • Open sea: the side position is steered only until 24.5 m; a gyro bias then moves the rover about 0.4 m by the end wall.
  • Crowds: groups of 3 to 5 at the pillar before the sign; shoulder-to-shoulder rows at the end wall.
  • No wheel encoders: all distances are commanded odometry. Slip on wet stones makes them too long.
  • Test the crowd cases for real, for example with team members standing at the sign pillar.
